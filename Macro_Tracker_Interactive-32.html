How to use this table

  • All weights are raw / pre-cooking unless a row says "cooked" or "canned". Chicken loses about 25% of its weight when cooked — if a recipe needs 115 g cooked, weigh about 145 g raw.
  • The Easy measure column is for when you have no scale handy — use it for rough planning, but a kitchen scale is far more accurate.
  • The IRON marker flags iron-rich foods. Pair these with vitamin C (lemon, orange, bell pepper) and avoid tea or coffee within 2 hours — important given the anemia picture in the lab reports.
  • The OMEGA-3 marker flags foods high in omega-3. Aim for fatty fish 2–3 times per week.
  • Small-portion items (oils, seeds, spreads) are listed per 10 g or per 30 g instead of 100 g, because nobody eats 100 g of olive oil. Check the basis line under each food name.
  • Tap any food to open the calculator: enter grams directly, a × 100 g multiplier, or pick an easy measure. The running total sits behind the floating button at the bottom corner.
  • The display button at the top of each table cycles through three views: (1) per 100 g — the default, uniform baseline for comparison; (2) DB portion — whatever the database defines (per 100 g, per 1 egg, per slice, etc.); (3) Easy measure — per the first easy unit (1 filet, 1 piece, 1 fruit). If a food has no easy unit defined, the Easy view shows per 100 g.
  • The smart sort button (↕) at the top of each table re-ranks items by what you actually care about: highest protein, highest protein with lowest carbs, lowest calories per gram, etc. The ranking uses the current display mode — so if you switch to Easy measure, the order reflects the actual grams shown.
  • Data sources: USDA FoodData Central (Foundation Foods and SR Legacy). Regional staples like baladi bread were cross-checked against published Middle Eastern composition tables. Values are typical averages and real products can vary by about 10%.
